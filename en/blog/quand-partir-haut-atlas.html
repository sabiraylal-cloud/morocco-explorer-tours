When to visit the High Atlas: seasons, snow and the best months

The question I hear most often, even before the price, is: “When should we come?” The answer depends on what you want to do. A day trip works almost all year round. A trek to the summit of Toubkal, at 4,167 metres, needs a little more care in choosing your moment.

Spring, the season of colour (April to June)

In spring the High Atlas valleys change completely. Barley grows on the terraces, fruit trees blossom around the villages and the walnut trees come back into leaf. The rivers run full with snowmelt and the waterfalls are at their best.

Higher up, winter does not let go straight away. Snow often stays on the ridges until May, sometimes longer on shaded slopes. In April the Toubkal ascent may still need crampons; from late May the trail is usually clear.

In the valleys the days are mild, around twenty degrees. Nights stay cool, especially at the refuge. For me, this is one of the two best periods for a trek.

Summer, an escape from the heat (July and August)

In July and August, Marrakech often goes above forty degrees. The mountains become a refuge: in Imlil, at 1,740 metres, the air is easier to breathe, and at the Toubkal refuge, at 3,207 metres, nights are cool even in midsummer.

This is the easiest season technically for the summit: no snow, a dry trail and no mountaineering equipment. But the sun is strong at altitude and afternoon storms are not unusual. So we start early, and we never cross a river that is rising after a storm: in the mountains, water rises very fast.

Summer is also the busiest time on the trails and in the refuges. For day trips, the Ourika valley draws many Marrakech families at weekends, so come on a weekday if you can.

Autumn, light and quiet (September to November)

Autumn is my other favourite season. The heat drops, the sky is often very clear and the golden evening light brings out the shape of the earthen villages. In the valleys it is harvest time: apples, walnuts and the last crops before winter.

The weather is usually stable until the end of October. In November the first snow can whiten the summits: it is beautiful, but the route and equipment then need adapting. September and October are perfect for both a four-day trek and a day trip.

Winter, the mountains under snow (December to March)

From December to March, Toubkal is usually covered in snow and ice. The ascent becomes a real mountaineering climb: crampons, an ice axe, experience on snow and a guide used to winter conditions. Days are short and the refuge is cold. It is a superb experience, but only for well-prepared walkers.

Below 2,500 metres, winter is still pleasant on a clear day. Snowy peaks make a spectacular backdrop for a day trip to Imlil, and after the winter rains the Ouzoud waterfalls carry an impressive amount of water. Just bring warm clothes: the sun is warm, but the shade is freezing.

Travelling during Ramadan

During the month of Ramadan, local people fast from sunrise to sunset. Treks and day trips carry on as normal: gîtes and families serve meals to travellers, and muleteers and guides work as usual, even if the rhythm of the day changes a little.

Out of respect, eat, drink and smoke discreetly in the villages during the day. In the evening, the breaking of the fast is often a warm moment, with harira soup, dates and honey pastries. In 2027, Ramadan is expected to start around 8 February; the exact date depends on the sighting of the moon and is only confirmed at the last moment.

In short: which month for what?

To climb Toubkal without technical equipment, aim for late May to October. The best months for walking are May, June, September and October. Winter suits experienced hikers looking for snow.

Day trips work all year: Ourika and Imlil for cool air in summer, Ouzoud in spring for the waterfalls, and any valley in autumn for the light. If you are unsure, look at our Toubkal trek by the classic route: the programme explains the level and the seasonal conditions.

A guide’s advice

  • Even in midsummer, pack a fleece and a windproof jacket: refuge nights are cold all year round.
  • In summer, set off early to walk in the cool and finish before any storms.
  • In winter, do not attempt the summit without crampons, an ice axe and an experienced guide.
  • Keep a spare day before your flight: changeable weather can delay the ascent.
  • High-altitude sun burns quickly: sun cream, sunglasses and a hat in every season.

Frequently asked questions

Can you climb Toubkal in winter?

Yes, but it is mountaineering: snow, ice, crampons and an ice axe. You need good experience walking on snow and a guide used to winter conditions.

What is the best month for a first trek?

June or September: the trail is clear, the days are long and the temperatures are pleasant.

Can I do day trips in winter?

Yes. The valley roads stay open, except during rare heavy snowfall. Dress warmly: it is much colder than in Marrakech.
