Preparing for a High Atlas trek: packing, fitness and altitude

A good trek starts at home, a few weeks before departure. You do not need to be an athlete to walk in the High Atlas, but a little preparation makes all the difference between an ordeal and real enjoyment. Here is what I tell everyone who travels with us.

What fitness level do you need?

On our Toubkal treks, days involve five to nine hours of walking, with up to 1,300 metres of ascent on the stage to the refuge, and a long summit day. So you need to be able to walk uphill for several hours, several days in a row.

The best training is simple: hikes with ascent, stairs and brisk walking, ideally with the pack you will carry. The Oued Zat trek is more moderate; the Azzaden valley loop is our most demanding route.

Altitude: no need to fear it, but don’t ignore it

Above 2,500 to 3,000 metres, the body gets less oxygen. Acute mountain sickness can affect anyone, even very fit people: headache, nausea, unusual tiredness, poor sleep. Our programmes climb gradually, with a night at the refuge at 3,207 metres before the summit.

The golden rules: walk slowly, “chwiya chwiya” as we say here, drink regularly and tell the guide straight away about any symptom. If the signs get worse, we go down: it is the most effective treatment, and there is no shame in turning back from a summit.

The mules carry, you walk light

On our treks, luggage is carried by mules with a muleteer. Pack a soft bag, not a hard suitcase, within the weight limit set out in our terms. The mules do not always take the same path as you and sometimes arrive after the group.

So you only carry a 20 to 30 litre daypack with what you need while walking: water, a warm layer, a rain jacket, sun protection, snacks, personal medication, documents and money.

Gîtes, refuges and camps

Depending on the route, you sleep in mountain gîtes, at the Toubkal refuge, with local families or in camp. Comfort is simple: dormitories or shared rooms, shared bathrooms, sometimes limited hot water and electricity. Nights at altitude are cold in every season.

Bring a warm sleeping bag, either your own or hired as we confirm when you book, earplugs for the dormitories, a head torch and a small quick-drying towel.

Water, money and documents

Do not drink spring or tap water without treating it: use purification tablets or a filter, or buy bottled water at the gîtes. A reusable bottle cuts down plastic in the mountains, where rubbish is hard to remove.

Withdraw dirhams in Marrakech before you leave: cash machines are rare or absent in mountain villages, and cards are almost never accepted. Keep your passport with you: it may be needed to register at accommodation.

Insurance is essential

Travel insurance is required on our treks. Check that it covers mountain trekking up to the trip’s altitude, as well as search, rescue and evacuation. In the High Atlas, evacuation is often on foot or by mule to the nearest road, and air rescue is not guaranteed. All the details are in our terms and conditions.

Your packing checklist

  • Broken-in hiking boots with good grip, and spare socks.
  • Three layers: breathable T-shirt, fleece, waterproof and windproof jacket.
  • Hat, gloves and a light down jacket for evenings and the summit, even in summer.
  • Sun hat, sunglasses, sun cream and lip balm.
  • Head torch, water bottle, purification tablets, small first-aid kit and blister plasters.
  • Walking poles: invaluable on the descents.
  • Dirhams in cash, your passport and proof of insurance.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a guide for Toubkal?

We strongly recommend one for safety, route-finding, weather and managing altitude. A mountain guide is included on all our treks.

Can I hire equipment locally?

Yes, in Imlil you can hire crampons, ice axes and sleeping bags. We tell you what you will need for the season.

How much money should I bring on the trek?

Nights and meals on the trek are included. Bring dirhams for drinks, tips and small purchases.
